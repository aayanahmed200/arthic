why now

the rules didn't slow down.
most teams' spreadsheets did.

CSRD just changed shape. The EU's 2026 Omnibus reform raised the threshold to more than 1,000 employees and €450 million in revenue — so most mid-sized companies aren't directly on the hook anymore. California's SB 253 works differently: it applies by revenue, not headcount, and the first companies have to report Scope 1 and 2 emissions by November 10, 2026. Neither rule has stayed still, and that's the real problem — the version you plan around today isn't guaranteed to be the version that applies next year.

Being out of direct scope doesn't mean being out of the conversation. Companies that are required to report need Scope 3 data from their own supply chain — so a mid-sized supplier with no sustainability team can still end up with an EcoVadis or CDP questionnaire on their desk and a customer waiting on an answer. arthic tracks which frameworks actually apply to you today, maps your existing data to whichever ones do, and stays current as the thresholds keep moving.

  • CSRD (EU) — >1,000 employees & >€450M revenue, post-Omnibus
  • SB 253 (California) — >$1B revenue; Scope 1–2 due Nov 10, 2026
  • ISSB / IFRS S1 & S2 — adopted or endorsed in 28+ jurisdictions, growing
  • plus whatever your largest customer's compliance team asks for next
