questions

frequently asked

Does my company actually have to comply with CSRD?
Maybe not anymore. The EU's 2026 Omnibus reform raised the threshold to more than 1,000 employees and more than €450 million in annual turnover — both, not either. That's a much higher bar than the original rule, and it moved most mid-sized companies out of direct scope. The catch: if a large customer or parent company is still in scope, they may ask you for the same Scope 1–3 data anyway.
What's the actual SB 253 deadline?
California's SB 253 applies to companies with more than $1 billion in total annual revenue doing business in the state — a revenue threshold, not a headcount one. The first reporting deadline, covering Scope 1 and 2 emissions, is November 10, 2026. Scope 3 reporting starts in 2027.
Is there a federal ESG disclosure law in the US?
No. The SEC's 2024 climate disclosure rule has never taken effect and remains in legal limbo. California's SB 253 (and the currently-paused SB 261) are the closest thing to a binding US mandate, and both are state-level.
My company doesn't meet any of these thresholds — why would I need this?
Because your customers might. Large companies that are in scope increasingly need Scope 3 data from their own supply chain, which means a mid-sized supplier with no sustainability team can still end up with an EcoVadis or CDP questionnaire and a customer waiting on an answer. arthic is built for that position — not legally mandated yourself, but expected to have the answer anyway.
What is ISSB, and does it apply to me?
The ISSB's IFRS S1 and S2 standards are a global sustainability-disclosure baseline, not a single law — 28+ jurisdictions had adopted or endorsed them as of 2026, with more in progress, including mandatory rules for listed companies already in effect in Japan. If you operate in, sell into, or raise capital from any of those markets, it's worth knowing what they'd ask for.
